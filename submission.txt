====================================================================
THE GAME IN SHORT
====================================================================

You are the Light Handler. Monsters have overrun the city square and only you
can see them. Your weapon is the EMW Machine, which fires different kinds of
light. Turn its colour wheel to match the ray to the enemy, dash through
danger, and hold the square through five eras.

The square is the same place in every era, but each era it is drawn with
less: fewer details, fewer colours. Each era also changes the rules of your
machine, so the same square and the same hands have to be played a different
way every time.

Five eras:

1. Golden Era     Bright sunshine, every detail. The full colour wheel:
                  Blue, Red and Green. Two minutes on the clock.
2. Cyberpunk Era  Dusk and neon. The Blue ray is taken away. You gain a dash.
3. Retro Era      Dark and dull. The machine is in overdrive and the wheel
                  locks and turns by itself. You gain a second dash and the
                  UV lens, which reveals enemies that hide.
4. Manga Era      Black and white. Only white light is left: a ring that
                  pushes enemies back and sends thrown things back at them.
5. Boss Era       The Prism. Every few attacks it switches the whole square
                  to another era, and your machine obeys that era's rules
                  until the next switch.

Then the ending.


====================================================================
CONTROLS
====================================================================

W A S D             Move
Mouse               Aim
Left mouse button   Fire (hold for the red laser; hold and release for
                    the green blob)
Q / E               Turn the colour wheel
F                   Switch mode: colour wheel, UV lens, white light
Space               Dash
P                   Pause (opens the comic book: eras, Field Guide,
                    the machine, settings)

The five rays:

Blue    A cone in front. Strong against swarms.
Red     A laser. Strongest up close. Strong against armour.
Green   A charged blob that slows what it hits and leaves a slowing pool.
        Strong against fast enemies.
White   A ring all round you. Pushes enemies away and turns thrown things
        round. Strong against swarms and throwers.
UV      A cone that reveals hidden enemies and halves their health.

The game never states these strengths during normal play. You learn them
from the comic sound words that pop up on a hit: a big burst for a strong
ray, a tiny grey word for a weak one.


====================================================================
THE THREE THEMES: LIGHT, COMIC, TWIST
====================================================================

LIGHT
Light is the weapon, and the whole combat system. The EMW Machine fires five
kinds of light (blue, red, green, white and ultraviolet), each with its own
shape and its own use, and each era changes which light you are allowed and
how it behaves. Light is also what the ending is about (see TWIST).

COMIC
The game is a comic book. The title screen is a comic cover. Each era is a
"page" drawn in a different comic style: a bright golden-age print, a neon
cyberpunk page, a dull retro page, and a black-and-white manga page. Hits
land with comic sound words. The pause screen is an open comic book you leaf
through with a page-turn animation, where each era you clear is "inked" as a
panel. The style of each page is not only a look: it changes how the weapon
works.

TWIST  (spoilers for the ending)
There were never any monsters. The Light Handler has schizophrenia, and it
has been getting worse, which is why the world loses detail and colour from
era to era. In the ending the detail comes back in plain daylight: the EMW
Machine is a pocket torch, the "monsters" were ordinary people and animals
in the square (pigeons, an ice-cream vendor, a cyclist, a jogger, a man in a
dark coat), and the boss was a police car. He is arrested for causing mild
annoyance to the public.

The twist changes the meaning of what came before. Every note in the Field
Guide is written so that it is literally true of the real thing as well, and
after the ending the Field Guide shows what each enemy really was. The
ending is played gently: the joke is how small the charge is, not his
illness.


====================================================================
SECRETS
====================================================================

There are four secrets, one in each of the first four eras. Each is a
hairline crack in a building wall. Only one kind of light breaks it, and it
must be a ray that era allows. Breaking it drops a health upgrade worth two
extra health blocks, which you keep for the rest of the run.

Golden Era     North side, in the shop front left of the town hall.
               Break it with Blue.
Cyberpunk Era  West wall, in the bookshop, low down near the bottom-left
               corner. Break it with Green.
Retro Era      North side, in the shop front right of the town hall.
               Break it with UV (press F for the UV lens).
Manga Era      East wall, in the pharmacy, low down near the bottom-right
               corner. Break it with White (stand next to the wall).

Stand close and aim at the crack. The wrong ray only makes a dull spark.
Found secrets are marked on the Eras page of the pause book.

The game also has a hidden mercy rule: after two deaths at the same
checkpoint, a heart is waiting near the start. It is never announced.


====================================================================
DEMO MODE AND GOD MODE (FOR JUDGES)
====================================================================

Both are in Settings. Open Settings with S on the cover, or with P during
play (last pages of the pause book). Both are off by default and are saved
in the browser.

DEMO MODE
Unlocks every era.
- On the cover, press 1 to 5 (or click an era) to start from that era, with
  the upgrades of the earlier eras already owned.
- During play, open the pause book and press 1 to 5 on the Eras page to jump
  to that era.
- The Field Guide also shows which rays each enemy is weak to, and the
  machine's page shows which enemies each ray is strong against. Normal play
  hides both.

GOD MODE
Nothing can hurt you. It can be switched on or off in the middle of an era.
Use it to see every era and the ending without risk.

Suggested for judging: play the first era normally to feel the intended
difficulty, then switch on God mode or Demo mode if you want to see the rest
quickly.


====================================================================
LENGTH
====================================================================

A complete loop, from the cover to the ending, is designed for 10 to 15
minutes: four timed eras of two minutes each, a boss fight of about two
minutes, and a one-minute ending, plus the cards between them. Dying
restarts from the last 45-second checkpoint, not the whole era, so the
length does not depend much on skill.


====================================================================
HOW IT WAS MADE
====================================================================

- Engine: Phaser 4 (JavaScript game framework), built with Vite and written
  in TypeScript. Runs in the browser as HTML5 / WebGL.
- Fresh codebase: all game code was written during the jam, starting from an
  empty Vite and Phaser project. No game templates, starter kits or
  pre-existing game code were used.
- Art: every sprite and background is pixel art defined in the game's own
  source code and drawn when the game starts. There are no image files.
- Music and sound: synthesised while the game runs by the game's own code,
  using the browser's Web Audio API. There are no audio files.
- The repository includes unit tests and an automated browser playthrough.
